# Personas

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **Status:** proto-personas from desk research. No guest research yet.

**Summary.** These are three working proto-personas for the guest, drawn from published reasons people use travel advisors and from what advisors probe for in discovery calls. All three are **hypotheses** until we talk to real guests. They exist so briefs can say *whose* problem they solve. The planner's job differs most between them in how much it must coordinate (one decision-maker vs. several) and how much a mistake costs (a milestone trip vs. a casual getaway). Update this file as research comes in, rather than starting new ones.

Labels: **[E]** evidence, **[IK]** industry knowledge, **[H]** hypothesis. Sources: [docs/product/research/advisor-discovery-call.md](research/advisor-discovery-call.md).

## 1. The time-poor planner of a complex trip

- **Who:** plans an international or multi-stop trip and has little time to research. **[H]**
- **Job:** "Give me a credible plan I can trust, without weeks of tabs." Values expertise and saved time. **[E]** Expert advice (52-53%) and time savings (52%) are the top reasons for using advisors; international and multi-destination trips are seen as advisor territory. [OpenJaw/Expedia, 2025-02-19](https://openjaw.com/newsroom/retail/2025/02/19/expert-advice-among-top-reasons-travellers-use-advisors-survey)
- **What they need from intake:** speed. They'll give when and who readily, but resent survey-like questions. **[H]**
- **What goes wrong:** "not knowing what they don't know" (seasonality, closures, entry rules). This is where the planner's web search earns its keep. **[IK]**

## 2. The milestone celebrator

- **Who:** an anniversary, honeymoon, big birthday or retirement trip, often a couple. **[H]**
- **Job:** "Make this one special and get it right; I'll only do it once." **[IK]**
- **What they need from intake:** for the occasion to be noticed and carried through. Advisors ask about milestones as a standard question. **[E]** [TMR, 2023-05-21](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/articles/25-Qualifying-Questions-to-Help-You-Nail-Your-Clients-Vacation-Plans)
- **What goes wrong:** a generic plan with nothing that marks the occasion, or budget assumed too low. **[H]**

## 3. The group or family coordinator

- **Who:** plans for a family (children of varied ages) or a group of friends, and is not the only decision-maker. **[H]**
- **Job:** "Find something everyone will enjoy and help me get the others to agree." **[IK]**
- **What they need from intake:** to have the party captured (ages, needs) so suggestions fit. Advisors always probe who's travelling and who else decides. **[E]** [Travelweek, 2025-06-17](https://www.travelweek.ca/blog/sphere-homebased/the-power-of-the-discovery-call/)
- **What goes wrong:** pace and activities that suit one member but not the rest, needs (mobility, diet) surfacing late, and no easy way to share the plan with co-deciders. **[H]**

## Not our persona (for now)

- **The price shopper** who wants the cheapest bookable option. Advisors screen these clients out. **[E]** [PTN, 2026-04-21](https://ptntravel.com/stop-wasting-hours-on-the-wrong-leads-your-guide-to-qualifying-travel-clients/). We can't book or quote live prices, so OTAs and metasearch serve them better. **[IK]**
- **The corporate traveller.** Different job (policy, duty of care, managed booking tools).
